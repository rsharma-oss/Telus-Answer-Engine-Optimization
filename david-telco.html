The pattern under the pattern

The single strategic shift a first-year president needs a view on. Not what's happening this quarter — what's compounding over your tenure.

A rising share of Canadian telecom buying decisions now begin inside an AI system — ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Google's AI answers — before a buyer visits any carrier site. The share is small today; industry analysts model it at 15–25% of research sessions by end of 2027, and TELUS's own web traffic already shows the referral pattern shifting.

What matters strategically is that the answer engines learn from what they see today. Whichever brand wins the citation now compounds the position later. TELUS has won that position early — this scorecard's 69/100 is the evidence — but the discourse layer shaping the answer is not brand advertising. It's Reddit, community forums, and comparison sites. Three moves that fit a first-year president's filter

Not tactics. Not budget asks. Three low-commitment moves that build governance, buy learning, and inform Q1 27 planning — before you commit to the bigger decisions the data will eventually surface.
Move 1 · Establish a monthly executive read of the answer surface
Governance · $0 incremental
Fifteen minutes of your executive-team meeting, once a month. One number (panel-wide TELUS share-of-answer). One chart (the trajectory). One question for the room ("what should we do about it?"). This is a governance instrument, not a marketing tool — it gives the Telco Division leadership a common view of where TELUS's answer position is holding, drifting, or moving. Costs nothing to add; hard to remove once your team is looking at it.
Why this fits the first-year filter New presidents are judged on whether their leadership team debates the same data. This is the cheapest, most legible move you can make to shape that debate — before you have to commit to a strategy.
Move 2 · Sponsor two low-cost Q4 pilots — learn what moves the number
Learning · Sub-$300K, Q4 26
Two named pilots this quarter, each with a defined hypothesis and a measurable signal. Pilot A: Home Office Fiber merchandising sprint — the highest-ceiling SMB gap (9 → 45 possible). Pilot B: A community-and-comparison-site presence sprint on two of the five gap queries. Both cap-and-measure: 8 weeks, panel signal reported to your executive team. If either moves the needle, Q1 27 planning has evidence, not opinions.
Why this fits the first-year filter A first-year president who bought learning cheaply before committing budget in Q1 has an entirely different quality of conversation with their CFO and board than one who committed and hoped. This is the classic "test before scale" move — legible, defensible, low-regret.
Move 3 · Direct a Bell competitive-narrative audit
Intelligence · 4-week sprint
Bell moved 34 → 46 in Rogers's mobile-heavy panel over two weeks — sustained, no reversal — while only drifting +3 in TELUS's broader panel over 30 days. That cross-panel signature says Bell shipped something mobile-shaped: pricing, promo, plan reposition, network narrative. A four-week competitive-intelligence sprint against the specific mobile queries where Bell is gaining tells you what it is before Q1 planning locks. Same instrument catches the next move too.
Why this fits the first-year filter New presidents that catch a competitive move early — and act on it before their peers do — accumulate credibility that compounds. This one is small, cheap, and has a specific signal you can point at when explaining the finding.
The through-line. None of these three moves require you to commit to a strategy. They install the instruments — a monthly executive read, two calibrated pilots, and a competitive intelligence rhythm — that generate the evidence a president can use in Q1 to make the bigger decisions with confidence. Cheapest possible way to start your term informed.

Methodology, for the room

The technical footer, for the CMO's team and any AEO Lead in the room.

The panel. Twenty realistic Canadian-telco buying questions — spanning consumer wireless, wireline, SMB, rural, and cross-cutting concerns like activation fees — are asked every Monday of five AI systems: ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Google AI Overview, and Google AI Mode. Results are appended to a durable, append-only tracking store. Panel v1 has been running since July 17, 2026 — 14 weekly pulls on record.

What "share of answer" means. For each question we count what percent of AI responses contain a substantive mention or citation of the brand across the full run set (5 models × N runs per week). A score of 34 for Bell means Bell appears in 34% of AI responses to the tracked questions in the measured window. Scores are directly comparable across brands.

Segment coverage. The twenty questions cover: consumer wireless (7), wireline & home internet (4), SMB wireless & wireline (5), rural business (1), and cross-segment concerns like activation fees, unexpected fees, and provider satisfaction (3). Weightings within the aggregate are equal by design — no editorial thumb on the scale.

Competitive framing. The five competitors reported here are the named Canadian brands the AI systems consistently return in response to the tracked questions. Flanker brands (Koodo, Fido, Virgin Plus, Public Mobile, Lucky, Chatr) are tracked separately when they surface but not included in this scoreboard.

What this scorecard is not. Not a search-engine ranking report. Not a brand-tracker survey. Not a predictive model. It is a weekly measurement of which brands the AI assistants are recommending to Canadian buyers, treated as strategic-intelligence input for the Telco Division executive team.
